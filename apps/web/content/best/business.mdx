Start with how your business pays its bills. A rewards card that fits predictable monthly expenses may be unsuitable if you need to carry balances through a seasonal cash-flow gap.

## Check payment requirements before rewards

Read whether the account requires payment in full or offers borrowing, and under what terms. Confirm personal guarantee requirements, employee card costs, spending controls and export options. A business name on the application does not by itself establish the liability terms.

## Example: a category cap changes the result

Imagine a card earning 3% on the first $20,000 of eligible expenses and 1% afterward. At $30,000 in that category, rewards total $700 before fees. An uncapped 2% alternative returns $600. A $150 annual fee on the first card would reverse the outcome to $550 versus $600. These are hypothetical terms.

## Separate financing and rewards decisions

Use actual operating expenses to compare cards; do not buy extra inventory just to reach a bonus threshold. Check [no-annual-fee comparisons](/credit-cards/no-fee) for lower fixed costs and [0% purchase planning](/best/0-apr) if financing is the main objective.

## How to use this guide

The calculations use hypothetical terms to show the trade-offs; they do not describe a live offer. Check the official issuer disclosures linked from each card profile for reward eligibility, fees and redemption conditions.
