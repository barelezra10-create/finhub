A secured card can help establish payment history, but the cash deposit ties up money. Choose an affordable deposit and manageable fees before looking at rewards.

## Questions to ask the issuer

Confirm the deposit requirement, resulting credit limit, annual and monthly fees, and which credit bureaus receive account activity. Ask when a deposit may be returned and whether an unsecured upgrade requires a new application. Do not assume an upgrade will happen on a fixed schedule.

## Example: deposit versus cost

If you place a hypothetical $300 deposit and pay a $35 annual fee, $335 leaves your available cash initially, but the two amounts serve different purposes. The deposit is collateral subject to return conditions; the annual fee is a cost. Include both in your budget while keeping them separate in the comparison.

## Does the deposit pay the monthly bill?

You still need to pay the statement. A deposit is not a substitute for those payments. Read [credit utilization](/glossary/credit-utilization) and compare the [first-card checklist](/best/first-time). Approval and the pace of credit improvement depend on your circumstances.
