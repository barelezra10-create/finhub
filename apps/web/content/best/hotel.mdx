A hotel card is useful only if its benefits fit stays you would otherwise purchase. A free-night certificate with restrictions can be worth much less to you than the highest advertised hotel price.

## Inspect the certificate before valuing it

Check expiration, eligible properties, award availability, category or points limits and whether a top-up is permitted. Verify required charges on the stay. Status benefits also depend on the program and property, so read the current terms.

## Example: value the stay you would buy

Suppose a hypothetical $150-fee card offers a certificate you can use instead of a $180 stay already in your plans. That provides $30 of value after the fee, before other costs or benefits. Replacing an unplanned $500 luxury stay does not automatically mean you saved $500.

## Compare a flexible alternative

If your destinations change often, compare a general [travel card](/credit-cards/travel) or [cash back approach](/best/cashback). Keep certificate value separate from points earnings so you do not count the same hotel stay twice.

## How to use this guide

The calculations use hypothetical terms to show the trade-offs; they do not describe a live offer. Check the official issuer disclosures linked from each card profile for reward eligibility, fees and redemption conditions.
