## Start with your application, not an approval promise

A credit-score label is not a lender’s commitment to approve an application. Review the specific product’s eligibility conditions and the information you will need to provide. Ask about income documentation, existing debts, state availability and any collateral requirement.

The [CFPB notes that lenders use different credit scores and models](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/credit-reports-and-scores/understand-your-credit-score/). Shopping actual offers can therefore produce different results from relying on a single score shown in an app.

## Check the process before submitting

Ask whether the initial check uses a soft inquiry and when a hard inquiry takes place. Confirm that the product is available where you live and that your planned use is allowed. Save the terms of any preliminary quote and review what remains subject to verification.

Do not assume all products are unsecured. [OneMain Financial](/loans/personal/onemain-personal-loan) and [Oportun](/loans/personal/oportun-personal-loan) describe collateral conditions for some offers. Read the relevant profile and agreement to understand what property, if any, secures repayment.

## Compare fees and the amount you receive

Compare APR, origination fees, net proceeds and total scheduled payments. Ask whether a fee is deducted from the disbursement and whether a longer repayment term changes the total cost.

For an arithmetic example, a $5,000 loan with a 5% fee deducted at funding deposits $4,750. You still owe the $5,000 principal plus the interest specified in the agreement. This is an illustration, not a lender quote.

## Ask about joint applications carefully

Check whether the specific product permits another applicant and what information is required from each person. Read both applicants’ repayment obligations. A joint application does not guarantee approval or a lower rate.

## Decide using your budget and written terms

Model the proposed payment alongside your existing obligations. If the payment does not fit, revisit the borrowing amount, timing or alternatives before committing. Ask the lender to explain any term you do not understand.
